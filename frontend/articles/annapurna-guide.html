Overview

The Annapurna Circuit is one of the world's legendary long-distance treks. Circling the entire Annapurna massif, it takes you through lush subtropical river valleys, dense rhododendron forests, high-altitude deserts, Tibetan-influenced villages and over Thorong La Pass at 5,416m — one of the highest trekking passes on earth.

What makes the Circuit special isn't any single destination — it's the extraordinary variety. In 14–18 days, you move from rice paddies and waterfalls to glacial valleys and stone deserts. Few treks on earth offer this range. Even seasoned Himalayan trekkers consistently call the Annapurna Circuit Nepal's most complete trekking experience.

2026 Update: The TIMS card situation is unclear — some checkpoints are enforcing it, others not. The ACAP permit is definitively required at $30. Our advice: get both permits in Kathmandu or Pokhara to avoid any checkpoint issues on the trail.

Is the Annapurna Circuit Right for You?

The circuit is classified as hard-moderate. The main challenge is Thorong La Pass — a 5,416m crossing that requires an early start (3–4am), altitude preparation, and good weather. The rest of the trek is long but manageable for anyone with reasonable fitness. Unlike EBC, the circuit is a point-to-point journey — you start in one place and end in another, which makes logistics slightly different.

Thorong La weather window is critical. The pass typically opens between 6am and 11am. Afternoon winds make crossing dangerous or impossible. Start no later than 4am from Thorong Phedi. If weather is bad, wait — do not attempt in poor visibility or strong winds.

Permits & Fees 2026

The permit situation for the Annapurna Circuit is simpler than EBC — only one permit is definitively required.

ACAP — Annapurna Conservation Area Permit

NPR 3,000 ~$25 USD

Mandatory for all trekkers. Available at NTB offices in Kathmandu or Pokhara, or at entry checkpoints. Funds conservation and trail maintenance in the Annapurna region.

TIMS Card (Situation Unclear in 2026)

NPR 2,000 ~$17 USD

Some sources say not enforced in 2026. Others report checkpoints still requiring it. Get it anyway in Pokhara — costs only $17 and avoids any checkpoint confusion.

Total permit cost: ~$42–50 USD — considerably cheaper than EBC permits. SAARC nationals pay NPR 1,000 for ACAP. Nepali citizens pay nothing.

Thorong La Pass — What to Expect

Crossing Thorong La at 5,416m is the defining moment of the Annapurna Circuit. It's a tough but non-technical crossing — no ropes, no crampons required in normal conditions. Here's what you need to know.

The Day-of Reality

  • Wake at 2:30–3am. Start walking by 3:30–4am latest. This is non-negotiable.
  • The climb from Thorong High Camp (4,925m) to the pass is about 3 hours of steep, exhausting ascent.
  • At the pass (5,416m), expect extreme cold, wind and thin air. It is survivable but brutal.
  • The descent to Muktinath (3,760m) takes another 3–4 hours — knees take a beating on the steep, loose trail.
  • Total day: 7–9 hours of hard trekking. This is the hardest day of the circuit by far.
  • If the pass is closed (heavy snow or storm), wait in Thorong Phedi. Never attempt in bad conditions.

Acclimatization at Manang is Critical

Spend at least one full rest day in Manang (3,500m) before attempting Thorong La. The optional hike to Ice Lake (4,620m) on your acclimatization day is excellent preparation — it gets your body used to higher altitude while returning to sleep lower. Skip this and you significantly increase your risk of AMS on the pass.

Circuit Highlights

  • Thorong La Pass (5,416m): The highest trekking pass on the circuit and one of the most rewarding challenges in Nepal
  • Muktinath Temple: Sacred pilgrimage site for both Hindus and Buddhists at 3,760m — a genuinely moving place
  • Kali Gandaki Gorge: The world's deepest gorge, flanked by Annapurna and Dhaulagiri massifs
  • Tilicho Lake (optional extension): One of the highest lakes in the world at 4,919m — adds 2–3 days but worth every step
  • Manang Village: Authentic high-altitude settlement with Manangi culture and incredible mountain backdrop
  • Poon Hill sunrise (end of circuit): Most circuits end via Ghorepani, giving you the famous Poon Hill sunrise as a final reward

Practical Tips

  • Start in Besisahar, not Bhulbhule: Road construction means many trekkers now start 2–3 hours further up the valley by jeep. The classic start is Besisahar — take a jeep from there to Chame if you're short on time.
  • Cash in Manang: Withdraw plenty of cash in Pokhara or Kathmandu. Manang has an ATM but it frequently runs out of cash during peak season. There are no ATMs above Manang.
  • Hot springs at Tatopani: After Thorong La, the hot springs in Tatopani are genuinely therapeutic. Don't rush past them.
  • Carry a down jacket to the pass: Even in October, Thorong La is freezing cold and windy at 4am. Your warmest layer is not optional.
  • Teahouse WiFi gets better after Manang: Starlink has been installed in several teahouses around Manang and Muktinath. Enjoy it — it doesn't last above 4,000m.
  • Jomsom to Pokhara flight books out fast: In October and April, the morning Jomsom–Pokhara flight fills up weeks in advance. Book before you leave Kathmandu or prepare for the bus.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the Annapurna Circuit cost in 2026?

Budget independent trekkers can complete the circuit for $700–$1,000 in-Nepal costs. Standard guided packages cost $1,200–$1,800. The big saving vs EBC is no Lukla flights — you travel by bus to the trailhead for ~$15–20.

Do I need a guide for the Annapurna Circuit?

As of 2026, guides are not legally mandatory for the Annapurna Circuit in the same way they are for other restricted routes. However, a guide is strongly recommended for Thorong La and altitude safety. The trail is well-marked but conditions can be challenging.

What is the best time to do the Annapurna Circuit?

October and November are the best months — post-monsoon clarity, stable weather, open passes. Spring (March–May) is second best, with rhododendron blooms at lower altitudes. Avoid monsoon (June–September) and January–February for the pass crossing.

How hard is Thorong La Pass?

Thorong La is hard — it's a 1,000m altitude gain from Thorong Phedi in the dark, followed by a 1,600m descent. It's not technical but it's exhausting. Start early (3–4am), go slow, stay hydrated. Most people who fail do so because of poor acclimatization at Manang or bad weather.

Can I do the circuit without reaching the pass?

Yes. If weather or health prevents a pass crossing, you can retrace your steps from Thorong Phedi to Manang and exit the circuit early. The partial circuit (Besisahar to Manang and back) is still a beautiful 10-day trek.
