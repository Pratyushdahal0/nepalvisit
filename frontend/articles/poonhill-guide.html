Overview

The Ghorepani Poon Hill trek is Nepal's most popular short trek — and for good reason. In just 4–5 days from Pokhara, you get jaw-dropping sunrise views over the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges, walk through ancient rhododendron forests, pass through traditional Gurung and Magar villages, and experience the warmth of Himalayan teahouse culture.

For first-time trekkers, this is the perfect introduction to the Himalayas. The trail is well-marked, teahouses are comfortable, and the maximum altitude of 3,210m at Poon Hill is high enough to feel genuinely alpine without the serious altitude risks of EBC or Annapurna Circuit.

2026 Guide Requirement: A licensed guide is now mandatory in the Annapurna Conservation Area as of recent Nepal trekking regulations. Checkpoints at Birethanti and Ghorepani verify this. Do not try to trek solo without a guide — you will be turned back.

Why Poon Hill is Special

The sunrise from Poon Hill viewpoint (3,210m) is one of the most photographed moments in Nepal. On a clear morning, you can see Annapurna South, Hiunchuli, Machhapuchhre (Fishtail), Dhaulagiri, and more than a dozen other peaks glowing golden in the first light. It's a 45-minute walk from Ghorepani in the dark, and every single person who makes it says it was worth it.

In spring (March–April), the rhododendron forests below Ghorepani burst into red and pink bloom — making this already beautiful trek visually extraordinary.

Permits & Fees 2026

ACAP Permit

NPR 3,000 ~$23 USD

Mandatory for all foreign trekkers in the Annapurna Conservation Area. Get it at NTB office in Pokhara before starting. Checked at Birethanti checkpoint.

TIMS Card

NPR 1,000–2,000 $8–17 USD

Required for safety tracking. Get at Nepal Tourism Board in Pokhara along with your ACAP permit. Both together take about 20 minutes to process.

Total permits: NPR 4,000–5,000 (~$35–50 USD). Always carry originals — checkpoints at Birethanti, Ghorepani and Tadapani will check them.

The Poon Hill Sunrise Experience

This is the moment the whole trek builds toward. Here's exactly what to expect:

  • Wake up time: 4:30–5am from Ghorepani. Your guide will wake you.
  • The climb: 45 minutes to 1 hour up stone steps through dark forest. Bring your headlamp — it's pitch black.
  • At the top: You'll be joined by dozens of other trekkers from Ghorepani teahouses. Arrive 15–20 minutes before sunrise to get a good spot.
  • What you see: The sun rises from behind the hills in the east. As it hits the snow-covered peaks, they glow orange, then gold, then brilliant white — Dhaulagiri (8,167m), Annapurna South, Hiunchuli, Machhapuchhre. On a clear day it stops you completely.
  • After sunrise: Return to Ghorepani for breakfast, then continue the trek to Tadapani.
  • Best months for clear views: October, November, March and April. Monsoon (June–September) often means clouds blocking the view.
Cloud cover warning: Clouds build rapidly from mid-morning in spring. If you want clear sunrise views, the earlier you summit Poon Hill the better. Don't sleep in.

Practical Tips

  • The Ulleri stairs: Day 2 involves roughly 3,000 stone steps up to Ghorepani via Ulleri. It's the hardest physical day of the trek. Go slow, take breaks, drink water. Your knees will thank you the next day.
  • Spring timing: If you go in March or April, rhododendron forests below Ghorepani are breathtaking. Peak bloom is usually late March to mid-April depending on year.
  • Teahouse booking: In October and April, Ghorepani fills up. Ask your guide to call ahead and book rooms — arriving at 4pm to find everywhere full is avoidable.
  • Ghandruk village: The Gurung Museum in Ghandruk is worth an hour of your time on Day 4. It explains the extraordinary history of Gurung soldiers in the British and Indian armies.
  • Jeep to Ulleri: If you're short on time or knees, a jeep can take you from Pokhara to Ulleri (skipping the Nayapul–Tikhedhunga section). This saves 2–3 hours on Day 1 but you miss a pleasant valley walk.
  • Layers: Ghorepani at night is cold — temperatures drop to 5–10°C even in October. Bring a warm fleece and down jacket for the evenings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Poon Hill suitable for beginners?

Yes — it's one of the best beginner treks in Nepal. The maximum altitude is 3,210m, which is high enough to feel Himalayan but low enough that serious altitude sickness is rare. The trails are well-marked and teahouses are comfortable. If you can walk 5–6 hours with a daypack, you can do Poon Hill.

How much does Poon Hill trek cost in 2026?

Budget independent trekkers (with guide) spend $250–$400 for a 5-day trek. Guided packages from Pokhara cost $450–$650 per person, including permits, transport, guide, accommodation and all meals. The 3-day version costs $165–$299 depending on package.

Do I need a guide for Poon Hill?

Yes — a licensed guide is mandatory in the Annapurna Conservation Area as of 2026. Checkpoints verify this. However, you don't need a porter — the trail is well-marked and most trekkers carry their own daypack. Your guide handles permits, teahouse bookings and safety.

What is the best time to do Poon Hill trek?

October and November for the clearest mountain views and stable weather. March and April for rhododendron blooms and warmer temperatures. December to February is cold but possible. Avoid June–September (monsoon — cloudy, leeches on the trail).

How do I get from Kathmandu to Pokhara for Poon Hill?

You can fly (25 minutes, ~$80–120 one way) or take the tourist bus (7–8 hours, ~$15–25) or public bus (~$10). Most trekkers fly to save time. The trek itself starts from Nayapul, 1.5 hours drive from Pokhara by bus or jeep.
