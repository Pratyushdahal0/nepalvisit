The Four Trekking Seasons

Nepal has four distinct seasons that affect trekking very differently. About 80% of trekkers visit during two narrow windows — Autumn and Spring. But each season has its own character, and the "wrong" season for EBC might be perfect for Upper Mustang.

2026 climate note: Nepal's weather patterns have shifted noticeably in the past decade. Monsoons are arriving later and ending later. October 2024 and 2025 both saw unexpected late-monsoon rain. Build buffer days into your itinerary regardless of month.

Autumn (September–November) — The Best Season

Autumn is the gold standard for Nepal trekking. The monsoon rains end in mid-September, leaving behind washed-clean air and crystal-clear mountain views. The valleys are still lush and green from summer rain, temperatures are moderate, and conditions are as close to perfect as they get in the Himalayas.

September

  • Early September: monsoon still possible, especially in the first two weeks
  • Late September (3rd week onwards): clears rapidly — trails are quiet, views are spectacular, leeches mostly gone
  • Great value: fewer trekkers than October, lower prices in some teahouses
  • Best for: getting ahead of the autumn rush while still getting excellent conditions

October — The Single Best Month

  • Post-monsoon clarity at its peak — deep blue skies almost every day
  • Temperatures ideal: 15–25°C at lower altitudes, manageable cold at high altitude
  • Dashain and Tihar festivals add cultural richness to village life on the trail
  • Busiest month on Nepal's trails — teahouses in Namche and Annapurna region fill fast. Book 3+ months ahead.
  • Best for: Everything — every trek in Nepal at its best

November

  • Early November: nearly as good as October with significantly fewer crowds
  • Late November: getting properly cold at altitude, some teahouses starting to close
  • Expert choice: early November gives great weather with 30–40% fewer trekkers than October peak
  • Best for: Trekkers who want October conditions without October crowds

Spring (March–May) — Second Best Season

Spring is Nepal's second-best trekking season and the time when the mountains feel most alive. Rhododendron forests — Nepal's national flower — burst into red and pink bloom from March to mid-April, and the Himalayan climbing expeditions (Everest, Annapurna, Dhaulagiri) are in full swing.

March

  • Winter is leaving — trails quieter and temperatures rising daily
  • First rhododendron blooms appear below 3,000m
  • Some high passes may still have residual snow from winter
  • Best for: Budget-conscious trekkers wanting spring conditions before the April rush

April — Second Best Month Overall

  • Peak rhododendron bloom on most routes — entire hillsides turn red and pink
  • Warmer days make high-altitude nights more manageable than autumn
  • Everest expeditions on the mountain — busy on the Khumbu trail
  • Visibility excellent in mornings; clouds build in afternoons (normal spring pattern)
  • Best for: Nature lovers, photographers, those who want warmth over crystal clarity

May

  • Getting hotter at lower altitudes — uncomfortable below 2,000m
  • Pre-monsoon clouds building from mid-May — views less reliable
  • Significantly fewer crowds than March–April
  • High altitude (above 4,000m) still excellent
  • Best for: Higher altitude treks (EBC, Annapurna Circuit) that stay above the heat
Spring afternoon clouds: Unlike autumn's consistently clear skies, spring mornings are clear but clouds often build by 2pm. Always summit viewpoints early. The Poon Hill sunrise works perfectly — you're up and back before the clouds arrive.

Winter (December–February) — For Experienced Trekkers

Winter trekking in Nepal is possible but requires experience and proper cold-weather preparation. The reward is extraordinarily clear skies, zero crowds, lower prices and a completely different kind of mountain experience.

What to Expect

  • Temperatures at EBC: -15 to -20°C at night, -5 to 5°C during the day
  • High passes like Thorong La (Annapurna) may be closed due to snow
  • Many teahouses above 4,000m close in January and February
  • Crystal-clear skies — some of the best mountain photography of the year
  • 30–40% cheaper flights and teahouses vs peak season
  • Essentially empty trails — you may have the mountains to yourself

Best Winter Treks

  • Poon Hill (3,210m): Doable with proper winter gear. Cold but manageable. Views can be spectacular with snow-covered peaks.
  • Kathmandu Valley day hikes: Mild winter conditions, cultural sites are open
  • Lower Mustang: Dry and accessible in winter — unique desert-mountain landscape
  • EBC (December only): Early December is still good. Late December and January: extreme cold, not recommended without winter mountaineering experience

Monsoon (June–September) — Generally Avoid

The monsoon is Nepal's rainy season and not recommended for most treks. That said, it's not a complete write-off — some regions are exceptional during monsoon.

Why Monsoon is Difficult

  • Heavy rain: 400–500mm per month from June to August
  • Slippery, muddy trails significantly increase risk of falls
  • Leeches are abundant on lower altitude trails
  • Mountain views obscured by cloud most of the day
  • Landslides can close roads and trails
  • Flights get cancelled frequently

Monsoon Exceptions — Where to Go

  • Upper Mustang (June–September): Sits in the rain shadow of the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges. When everywhere else is soaking, Mustang is dry and warm. Nepal's best summer trek.
  • Dolpo region: Also in rain shadow — dry and accessible in monsoon
  • Kathmandu cultural tourism: Temples, markets, cooking classes, pottery — great if you want culture over trekking

Budget tip: teahouse accommodation is 30–40% cheaper during monsoon on popular routes. If you're doing Lower Mustang or Kathmandu sightseeing, monsoon is actually great value.

Which Trek for Which Season

Best treks in Autumn (Oct–Nov)

  • Everest Base Camp — October is the single best month for EBC
  • Annapurna Circuit — Thorong La fully open, weather perfect
  • Langtang Valley — quieter than EBC, equally beautiful conditions
  • Manaslu Circuit — October–November is the only time it's fully accessible

Best treks in Spring (Mar–May)

  • Poon Hill — peak rhododendron bloom March–April is unmissable
  • Annapurna Base Camp — beautiful rhododendron forest ascent in spring
  • EBC — April is excellent, May still works above 4,000m
  • Langtang — spring blooms, quieter than autumn

Best treks in Winter (Dec–Feb)

  • Poon Hill — manageable with proper winter gear, clear views
  • Ghandruk loop — low altitude, mild winter conditions
  • Lower Mustang — dry, accessible, unique landscape
  • Kathmandu Valley hikes — mild, culturally rich

Best treks in Monsoon (Jun–Sep)

  • Upper Mustang — rain shadow, dry conditions, unique landscape
  • Dolpo region — also in rain shadow, remote and spectacular
  • Kathmandu cultural tourism — avoid mountains, explore culture

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the absolute best month to trek in Nepal?

October. Post-monsoon clarity, near-perfect weather, open passes, Dashain and Tihar festivals adding cultural richness. The downside is crowds and higher prices. If you want October weather without October crowds, target early November instead.

Is April or October better for Nepal trekking?

October wins for clarity and reliability. April wins for warmth, rhododendron blooms and fewer crowds. If you want the best mountain photos with the clearest skies — October. If you want lush, colorful trails with warmer nights — April. Both are excellent choices.

Can I trek Nepal in December?

Yes — early December (1st to 15th) is excellent. Clear skies, few trekkers, cold but manageable with proper gear. Late December means very cold temperatures above 3,500m and possible pass closures. For Poon Hill or lower altitude treks, December is fine with good layering.

What is the rainy season in Nepal?

The monsoon runs from June through mid-September. June and July are the wettest months with heavy daily rainfall. Most trails are slippery, views are obscured and leeches are active. Upper Mustang and Dolpo are the main exceptions — they sit in rain shadows and stay dry during monsoon.

How has climate change affected Nepal trekking seasons?

Noticeably. Monsoons are arriving later and ending later — the 3rd week of September (rather than 1st) is now a more reliable start to autumn season. October 2023 and 2024 saw unexpected late-monsoon rainfall. Winters are slightly warmer at lower altitudes but extreme cold events above 5,000m are becoming more common. Always check current conditions through local guides before finalizing dates.
